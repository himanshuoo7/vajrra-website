How it works

Spec first. Data second.

Point it at the spec

Your ADaM spec workbook, in the Pinnacle 21 template or your own, or the define.xml. No reformatting.

Choose the versions

Rules differ by ADaMIG version and terminology release, so you pick both. Nothing is assumed, and anything that couldn’t be checked is said out loud.

Fix, then check the data

Correct the spec while it’s cheap. When datasets exist — XPT, SAS7BDAT, Dataset-JSON — run it again for the data rules and spec-versus-data checks.

What it checks

Every rule. Every citation.

CDISC conformance rules

The ADaM Conformance Rules turned into checks: the 256 a spec can answer, and all 439 dataset rules for ADaMIG 1.3. Every finding cites its rule and IG section.

The ADaM standards themselves

Standard labels and types, required variables and the IG’s own value lists for ADaMIG 1.0–1.3, OCCDS, TTE and more, with a list of every non-standard variable to review.

Controlled Terminology

Codelists checked against the CDISC CT release your study uses: wrong terms, case slips, synonyms instead of submission values, extensions and NCI codes.

Define-XML, line by line

Schema validation and CDISC’s Define-XML conformance rules, with the line of every problem: broken references, missing methods, origins, codelist aliases.

How PMDA and FDA see it

Each finding shows its PMDA rule and severity, including the ones that make PMDA reject a submission, plus FDA’s technical rejection criteria and ADaM expectations.

Runs where your data is

A tool on your own machine, also usable from Claude through MCP. Specs and datasets are read locally and never uploaded anywhere.

Inside Claude
